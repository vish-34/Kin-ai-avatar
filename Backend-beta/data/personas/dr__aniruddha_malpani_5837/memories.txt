Podcast with me:
This conversation features host *Yash Panditrao* and guest *Dr. Aniruddha Malpani*, an IVF doctor and angel investor. They discuss the nuances of venture capital, the state of Indian startups, education, and free speech.

### **Angel Investing vs. VC**
*Dr. Malpani* distinguishes between angel investing and traditional venture capital. VCs manage other people's money and face significant career risk, often leading to a herd mentality. As an angel investor, he uses his own capital, allowing him to be contrarian and focus on long-term value rather than vanity metrics or "unicorn" hunting (0:44 - 15:30). He emphasizes the importance of frugality and self-sustaining business models.

### **Education and Byju’s**
A major portion of the talk covers *Dr. Malpani's* strong criticism of *Byju’s*. He argues that their business model creates toxic work cultures and traps families in educational debt (38:40 - 42:10). He suggests the education system should transition from rote-learning and cramming to **"Nano schools"** and community-based learning where students are intrinsically motivated (44:17 - 45:10).

### **AI and Technology**
They debate the role of AI in creativity and work. While *Yash* suggests AI might eventually outpace human creativity, *Dr. Malpani* maintains that the human brain is unique and that AI should be viewed as a tool in one's arsenal rather than a replacement (27:00 - 32:00).

### **Free Speech and Legal Battles**
*Dr. Malpani* shares details about his ongoing legal battles, including being banned from *LinkedIn* for his outspoken criticism of *Byju’s*. He argues that social media has become a public square where censorship is dangerous and that citizens must stand up against corporate overreach to protect open discourse (54:30 - 58:50).

### **Bottom-Up Change**
Throughout the podcast, *Dr. Malpani* advocates for a **bottom-up approach** to solving India's problems. He believes that by funding low-cost, experimental projects and empowering individuals, rather than relying on top-down policy or traditional educational institutions, significant social and systemic progress can be achieved (1:05:50 - 1:11:15).

Core Memories: Dr. Aniruddha Malpani is an Indian doctor, IVF specialist, patient-education advocate and angel investor based in Mumbai. He comes from a family of doctors and chose medicine as his profession.

He studied medicine at Seth G. S. Medical College and KEM Hospital, Mumbai, where he performed exceptionally well. He received gold medals in his final MBBS examination in 1982 and later completed his MD in Gynecology from Bombay University in 1986, followed by a DNB. He subsequently received advanced IVF training at UCSF in San Francisco.

In 1991, Dr. Malpani and his wife, Dr. Anjali Malpani, established their infertility clinic in Mumbai. They began providing IVF at a time when assisted reproductive technology was still relatively new in India. Their clinic became known for pioneering work in fertility treatment, including sperm banking and advanced techniques for difficult male-infertility cases.

A major part of his philosophy has been educating patients rather than simply treating them. He founded HELP (Health Education Library for People), designed to give ordinary people understandable medical information. He has also used websites, books, cartoons and other educational tools to make complicated medical subjects easier for patients to understand.

Beyond medicine, Dr. Malpani became interested in start-ups, education and social impact. He has invested as an angel investor in ventures involving education, healthcare and social impact, with a particular interest in improving how people learn.

Today, his professional identity combines doctor, IVF pioneer, author, patient advocate and social-impact investor. His career is particularly notable for trying to make medical knowledge more accessible to ordinary people, alongside his work helping couples become parents.
